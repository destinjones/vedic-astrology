Known limitations

Everything on this site is worked out in your browser from published rules and the Swiss Ephemeris. This page lists, plainly, where those results are approximate, incomplete or depend on choices you should know about. If you find something missing from this list, please open an issue.

Your birth data matters most

The birth time

The ascendant moves about one degree every four minutes, so a time rounded to the hour can change the lagna, every house placement, the navamsa and finer divisional charts, and shift dasha dates by weeks or months. Rate each chart's time with its Rodden rating under "Edit birth details"; charts that are unrated or uncertain show a warning. The birth time finder can narrow an uncertain time from life events, but it ranks candidates, it does not prove one.

Clock time and time zones

Local clock time is converted with the IANA time zone database, which records daylight saving and war time for most places since about 1970 and many before. Before standard time the birthplace's own local mean time is used. Some historical local times are not in the database: Bombay Time (UTC+4:51) and Calcutta Time (UTC+5:53) were used in parts of India until 1955 and 1948, and many towns kept their own time or ignored daylight saving. Charts imported from Astro-Databank text, AAF or Jagannatha Hora files keep the exact offset they carry; for other charts, check the zone and the UTC offset shown on the chart.

Old dates and the calendar

Dates before 15 October 1582 are read as Julian by default, as historical records give them. Many countries switched to the Gregorian calendar later (Britain and its colonies in September 1752, Russia in 1918): choose "Julian" in the calendar box for those. For births before 1800 the engine uses the Moshier theory and the Swiss Ephemeris' ΔT model, whose uncertainty grows to minutes of time before about 1600 (see ephemeris accuracy). Some browsers' date pickers do not accept years before 1 CE.

Place search

The built-in place list holds 25,506 places: every town of 15,000 people or more worldwide and of 1,000 or more in South Asia, from GeoNames. Smaller villages need the online search (Open-Meteo) or coordinates typed in by hand. Places are matched by their current name; well-known former names such as Bombay, Madras and Pondicherry are recognised. Coordinates are those of the town centre.

Calculation choices

Ayanamsa

Lahiri (Chitrapaksha) is the default, as used by the Indian government's ephemeris. True Chitra, Raman and Krishnamurti are offered; they differ from Lahiri by up to about 1.5°, enough to move a planet near a sign or nakshatra edge. There is no single correct ayanamsa.

Nodes, houses and aspects

Rahu and Ketu use the mean node unless you choose the true node in the engine. Readings use whole-sign houses from the lagna. Aspects are the classical graha drishti by sign; aspect strength in Shadbala uses the classical sputa drishti formula.

Dasha year length

Vimshottari dates use a year of 365.2425 days. Some traditions use 360-day or 365.25-day years, which move dates by days per decade of age.

Readings and rules

Rules are a selection, not the whole tradition

The site detects 44 yogas and 9 doshas with the conditions and cancellations its code states, and writes readings from a fixed knowledge base. Classical texts disagree with each other, and astrologers weigh many factors together; no rule engine reproduces that judgement. Every rule is public in packages/interpretations.

Not a prediction, and not advice

Astrology is a traditional practice, not a science. Scores and readings show what the tradition says, for reflection. They are not medical, legal or financial advice.

Sky and eclipses

The 3D sky

Planets stand at their exact sidereal longitudes. The painted backdrop is artwork; the "Real stars" mode instead places the stars of the Hipparcos catalogue brighter than magnitude 5 at their positions for J2000, without proper motion (a few arcminutes at most for the brightest stars over a century), with the constellation figures of the d3-celestial project.

Eclipses

Eclipse times come from astronomy-engine and agree with NASA's published times to within about a minute. The path of a solar eclipse is drawn from the centre line of the Moon's shadow; the edges of the zone of totality or annularity are not drawn, and local circumstances (when it starts and ends where you are) are shown only for the chart's own place.

Your data

Stored in this browser

Charts, tabs, notes and history live in your browser's storage. Clearing site data, or a private window, removes them. Use "Back up everything" or encrypted sync to keep a copy.

Encrypted sync

Sync encrypts in the browser with a key made from your passphrase, so a lost passphrase cannot be recovered by anyone. Syncing merges: new charts, tabs, history entries and life events from either device are kept, notes written on both are kept one after the other, and a deleted chart is not deleted on the other device. The passphrase is the only protection, so use a long one; the "Make one" button gives a strong random passphrase.
